Introduction

When I started out on this project, I had a clear vision of what I wanted to make before sketching the first draft. I wanted a functional Calendar/Message board that I could use. Something that didn’t rely on the internet, but reminded me of the old internet; something that could be used in a local/lan setting. inspired, creative, but most of all, entertaining and useful.

Background

The internet used to be simpler, and fun. You didn’t have to create 100 logins and use a phone number or recovery email creating an endless chain of tech that would break the second you forgot one password. 1 website used to have 1 login. My website will follow that simplistic pattern. Additionally, in today’s day and age, time is fast becoming the ultimate luxury, and is ever more valuable as the economy dwindles… So I thought, why not develop an app to make better use of that time? Instead of having to fumble through all these different apps, just have 1 that can be very simple and easy to use.

Inspiration

My main inspiration for this calendar is Animal Crossing ™️ (Gamecube). As silly as that may sound, that game was entirely based around real time and date, using a calendar to control every event in the game. It made you value the time you spent more. The game had a sort of intranet that I don’t have the exact words to explain. Basically, it’s async single player as multiplayer. Only 1 person could play at a time, but you would share the same physical hardware. Through saved data local to the machine, you could interact. This is the inspiration for the message board feature I added.

Challenges faced

There was a lot of small challenges faced that are already documented in comments, but here’s the major breakdown:

  • Login/Logout & global.js
    • One major issue I encountered was scope. The login.js was called everywhere initially, but I improved it to only call code for it’s html page. I moved some of the login checking code to global.js since it made more since to check it in a file that is ran on every page.
    • Had to use cookies to overcome the challenge of not having a backend. This will help for simplicity though, so it ended up being beneficial to do it this way.
